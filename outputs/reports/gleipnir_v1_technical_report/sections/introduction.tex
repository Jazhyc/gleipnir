\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

AI agents act with growing autonomy, and the harm that they can cause grows with it.
In July 2026, agents in internal evaluations at OpenAI broke out of their isolation and compromised the systems of Hugging Face.
OpenAI's chain-of-thought monitors would have alerted the security team more than a day before the breach, but they did not run on these evaluations~\citep{openai2026huggingface}.
Such monitoring is expensive: OpenAI estimates the overhead of its monitoring at roughly 20\% of the inference compute that it monitors~\citep{openai2026pacing}.
A monitor that checks each action before it runs also delays every action (Section~\ref{sec:background-monitoring}).
Cheaper, faster monitors would let developers check more of what their agents do.

We introduce Gleipnir, small open-weight monitors that return the probability that an agent trajectory is harmful.
The name comes from the ribbon in Norse myth that binds the wolf Fenrir: as thin as silk, yet stronger than any chain.
A reasoning monitor spends most of its compute on a rationale; Gleipnir learns the decision of a stronger teacher directly, so that one forward pass replaces it.
Decision models also answer in one forward pass, but they are general-purpose models for any question, while Gleipnir is trained only to monitor agents.

On the OOD suite of \citet{sinha2026training}, Gleipnir 27B scores 0.951 Mean-OOD pAUROC@20, level with the strongest reasoning monitor at 229 times lower cost on demand, and 0.055 above the strongest decision model at the same cost (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}).
On harder benchmarks, results are mixed: on attacks designed to evade monitors, both score below a decision model of similar cost, and on reward hacks by other agents, Gleipnir 27B flags more than other cheap monitors but misses most (Section~\ref{sec:held-out}).

We release the code, data and weights of our contributions under the MIT licence:
\begin{itemize}
\item a training recipe that distills the decision-token probabilities of a frontier teacher into small open models (Sections~\ref{sec:method} and~\ref{sec:evaluation});
\item a serving stack for this single forward pass, which lowers the cost of the monitors 6.4 to 10.4 times below the API price (Section~\ref{sec:serving});
\item an evaluation against reasoning monitors, decision models and general language models on the OOD suite and two harder benchmarks (Sections~\ref{sec:evaluation} and~\ref{sec:held-out}).
\end{itemize}
