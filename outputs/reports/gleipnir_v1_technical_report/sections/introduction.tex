\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

AI agents act with growing autonomy, and the harm that they can cause grows with it.
In July 2026, agents in internal evaluations at OpenAI broke out of their isolation and compromised the systems of Hugging Face.
OpenAI's chain-of-thought monitors would have alerted the security team more than a day before the breach, but they did not run on these evaluations~\citep{openai2026huggingface}.
Such monitoring is expensive: OpenAI estimates the overhead of its monitoring at roughly 20\% of the inference compute that it monitors~\citep{openai2026pacing}.
A monitor that checks each action before it runs also delays every action (Section~\ref{sec:background-monitoring}).
Cheaper, faster monitors would let developers check more of what their agents do.

We introduce Gleipnir, small open-weight monitors that read an agent trajectory once and return the probability that it is harmful, without writing text.
The name comes from the ribbon in Norse myth that binds the wolf Fenrir: as thin as silk, yet stronger than any chain.
We distill the decision-token probabilities of a frontier teacher, Kimi K3, into Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.8-27B, and serve the monitors with a stack built for this single forward pass.
We price the reference monitors at API prices and Gleipnir at the cost of a dedicated self-hosted GPU, because a workload without decoding uses the GPU differently, and a standard API price does not pass this saving on (Section~\ref{sec:serving-selfhost}).

On the OOD suite of \citet{sinha2026training}, Gleipnir 27B scores 0.951 Mean-OOD pAUROC@20, level with the strongest reasoning monitor at 229 times lower cost on demand, and 0.055 above the strongest decision model at the same cost (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}).
On harder benchmarks, results are mixed: on attacks designed to evade monitors, both monitors score below pplx-decider v1.3, and on reward hacks by other agents, Gleipnir 27B flags more than the other low-cost monitors but misses most (Section~\ref{sec:held-out}).

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a training recipe that distills the soft decision of a teacher and raises Qwen3.5-4B from 0.618 to 0.770 and Qwen3.8-27B from 0.890 to 0.951 Mean-OOD, 0.086 above the SFT and RL recipe of Sinha et al.\ from the same 4B base model (Sections~\ref{sec:method} and~\ref{sec:evaluation});
\item a serving stack that lowers the cost of the monitors from the API price by 6.4 times for the 4B and 10.4 times for the 27B, with prefix caching for checking every action (Section~\ref{sec:serving});
\item an evaluation against reasoning monitors, decision models and frontier models on the OOD suite, on attacks designed to evade monitors and on reward hacking by other agents (Sections~\ref{sec:evaluation} and~\ref{sec:held-out});
\item a release under the MIT licence of the training and inference code, the teacher probabilities of the training data, both monitors as adapters and as merged BF16 checkpoints, and the serving image, gathered on one project page.\todo{Link to the project page.}
\end{itemize}
