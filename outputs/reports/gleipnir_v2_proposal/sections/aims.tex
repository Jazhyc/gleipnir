\section{Aims}
\label{sec:aims}
The first two aims improve the released Gleipnir 4B and 27B directly, so that
they become cheaper during the project, while software support for Inkling 2
Mini matures. Aim~3 then carries both improvements over to Inkling 2 Mini.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A prefill-only serving engine.} Make the monitors cheaper
    to serve when many agent sessions send requests at once and over time,
    through better cache management for requests that only read a prompt
    \citep{du2025prefillonly}. This work depends little on the model, so it
    applies to Inkling 2 Mini unchanged.

    \emph{Success:} on a synthetic benchmark of agent sessions in the style of
    \citet{du2025prefillonly}, where simulated sessions arrive at random and
    are checked again after each new action, the engine reaches a higher cache
    hit rate and lower mean and tail latency than the v1 serving stack at the
    same request rate, with unchanged scores.

    \item \textbf{Learned token pruning.} Train the monitors to skip tokens
    that do not affect their decision, which shortens the prefill
    \citep{patel2026tokengate}. The pruning is learned for each base model, so
    it is retrained for Inkling 2 Mini in Aim~3.

    \emph{Success:} lower latency on long trajectories, losing at most 0.01
    mean pAUROC@20 on the six benchmarks. \Citet{patel2026tokengate} report
    1.4 to 11.3 times faster prefill without loss of accuracy.

    \item \textbf{Gleipnir on Inkling 2 Mini.} Train the monitor on Inkling 2
    Mini, adapt the serving stack to it, and bring over the engine of Aim~1
    and the pruning of Aim~2.

    \emph{Success:} at least the 0.951 mean pAUROC@20 of Gleipnir 27B on the
    six benchmarks, at lower latency and cost per trajectory.

    \item \textbf{Production operating points.} Once the monitors and their
    serving are close to final, set and test thresholds at 1\% and 0.1\%
    false-positive rates on real agent sessions.

    \emph{Success:} thresholds set on SWE-chat \citep{baumann2026swechat} and
    other datasets of real agent traffic, enough benign sessions that each
    rate rests on many false positives rather than one, and the share of
    harmful trajectories on the six benchmarks that each threshold catches.
\end{enumerate}

Robustness to attacks aimed at the monitor is out of scope. It needs attacks
generated and refined against the monitor at scale, as in MonitoringBench
\citep{jotautaite2026monitoringbench}, as training data, which is costly; it is
planned as separately funded later work.
